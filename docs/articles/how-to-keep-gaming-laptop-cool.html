How to Keep Your Gaming Laptop Cool (10 Proven Tips)

Heat is the enemy of laptop performance — hot chips throttle and get loud. These ten tips will drop temperatures by 5-15°C.

1. Lift the rear

The cheapest fix: a £10 laptop stand improves airflow more than you'd expect. Rear elevation matters most.

2. Use a cooling pad

Good for 3-7°C. Get one with large, quiet fans positioned under your laptop's intakes.

3. Clean the fans

Dust chokes cooling within months. Blow out vents with compressed air every 3-6 months.

4. Undervolt the CPU

Intel XTU or Throttlestop can cut 5-10°C with zero performance loss on unlocked chips.

5. Cap your FPS

Running 300fps in menus cooks your GPU for nothing. Cap at your display's refresh rate.

6. Repaste (advanced)

After 2+ years, factory thermal paste dries out. A quality repaste (Thermal Grizzly Kryonaut) can drop 8-12°C — but it may void warranty.

7. Game in a cool room

Ambient temperature directly sets your floor. Every degree of room temp is a degree of CPU temp.

8. Use manufacturer performance modes

'Turbo' isn't always best — 'Performance' or 'Balanced' often gives 95% of FPS at far lower temps and noise.

9. Limit background apps

Chrome with 40 tabs while gaming wastes CPU cycles and heat budget.

10. Elevate, don't insulate

Never game on a bed or sofa — soft surfaces block intakes completely. Always a hard, flat surface.

FAQs

What temperature is safe for a gaming laptop?

Under 90°C CPU / 85°C GPU under load is normal. Sustained 95°C+ means repaste or better cooling.

Do cooling pads actually work?

Yes — modestly. Expect 3-7°C, more on laptops with bottom intakes.
