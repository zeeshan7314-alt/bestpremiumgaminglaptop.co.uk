eGPU vs Gaming Laptop in the UK — Which Makes Sense in 2026?

Already own a thin laptop? An external GPU enclosure could add gaming power — but the maths rarely works in 2026. Here's why.

The cost problem

A decent enclosure (£250-350) + GPU (£400-600) = £650-950 — the price of a full RTX 4050/4060 gaming laptop with screen, keyboard and portability included.

The performance penalty

Thunderbolt 3/4 bottlenecks cost 10-25% of the GPU's performance versus the same card in a desktop.

When eGPU makes sense

You already own a powerful thin-and-light (e.g. a MacBook or ultrabook for work) and only game docked at a desk. One machine, two roles.

The better alternative

For most people, selling the old laptop and buying a proper gaming laptop (like our sub-£1,000 picks) gives more performance for similar money.

FAQs

Does eGPU work with any laptop?

You need Thunderbolt 3/4 or USB4 with PCIe support — check your specs. Many budget laptops lack it.

Is eGPU worth it in 2026?

Rarely for new buyers. It only makes sense if you already own a compatible premium ultrabook.
